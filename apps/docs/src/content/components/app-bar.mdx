## Purpose

The app bar is the strip across the top of a Tecton application: the logo and app name, the tabs
people switch between, and room on the right for the app's own buttons. In a micro-frontend setup
the shell typically owns it.

`AppBar` is the Tecton App Bar from the Figma design, with the looks and behaviour of the Tecton
`<wc-app-bar>` drop-in. It replaces the MUI `AppBar` and draws the Tecton row itself (no `Toolbar` to
add). Compose it from its parts, which follow MUI's `Tabs` and `Tab`:

| Part            | What it is                                                                        |
| --------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `AppBarBrand`   | The product mark (`logo`) and the app name (children)                             |
| `AppBarTabs`    | MUI `Tabs` props, plus `onAdd` for the add button and `attached`                  |
| `AppBarTab`     | MUI `Tab` props, plus `onClose` for a close button and `tags`; icon-only for Home |
| `AppBarActions` | Your buttons, at the end of the bar                                               |

## Basic app bar

Select a tab, close the active one with its close button, or add one with +.

<Demo name="TectonAppBar" />

**Your page owns the tabs.** `AppBarTabs` calls `onChange(event, value)` when a tab is selected and
`onAdd(event)` for +; a tab calls its own `onClose(event)`. The page updates its tabs and `value`;
the bar never changes them on its own. When the selected tab is closed, select the tab before it, as
the examples do.

## Branding

`AppBarBrand` shows its `logo` next to the app name: a Tecton `Icon`, an `<img>` or any element.
Without a `logo`, a placeholder square shows (the Tecton design's generic app icon); `logo={null}`
shows none.

## Tabs

An `AppBarTab` takes the MUI `Tab` props: `value`, `label`, `icon`, `disabled`, `sx` and the rest.
Without a `label` it is an icon-only cell, such as Home; give it an `aria-label`. A Tecton `Icon` shows
filled on the selected tab and outlined on the others, unless you set its `variant`. `tags` adds
small chips after the label, and `onClose` gives the tab a close button, shown while it is selected.

### Adding tabs

`onAdd` on `AppBarTabs` shows the add button after the tabs. To cap the number of tabs, leave
`onAdd` out once the cap is reached: add two tabs below and the + goes away; close one and it comes
back.

<Demo name="TabLimitsAppBar" />

### Many tabs

When the tabs outgrow the bar, the tab strip scrolls, while the branding, the add button and the
actions keep their place. The strip is MUI's scrollable `Tabs`, as in the Tecton drop-in: MUI's
40px scroll arrows, each shown only while the strip can scroll its way (a hidden arrow takes no
room). A click scrolls by the tabs that fit, and a newly selected or added tab is scrolled into
view, both over the theme's `transitions.duration.standard` (with reduced motion the strip jumps).
When the start arrow shows, the tabs stay where they are. Below the theme's `sm` breakpoint (600px)
there are no arrows, as in MUI: the strip scrolls by touch, wheel or keyboard.

<Demo name="ManyTabsAppBar" />

## Actions

`AppBarActions` sits at the end of the bar and centres its content: icon buttons, text buttons, a
menu button or an avatar. The tabs scroll to make room for it. When even the narrowest tab strip
(the selected tab between its two arrows, or without them below 600px, and the add button) leaves
too little room, the actions collapse into an overflow (⋮) button, first to last, so put the
primary one last. The button opens a tray that shows them as they are; a click on a command in it
closes it. It works like the [Heading](/components/heading)'s actions, and takes
`slotProps.overflowButton` and `slotProps.overflowTray` the same way. Pass the actions as separate
children (fragments are fine) so that they collapse one by one. Other content, such as a [Menubar](/components/menubar), can go
anywhere in the bar.

## Tabs that open into the content

With `attached` on `AppBarTabs`, the selected tab opens into the content under the bar, like a
folder tab: the bar's bottom line stops under it and the tab takes the content's color. It works in
both tiers and suits a secondary bar that switches the views of a page. `attached` alone uses the
page background; when the content below has another color, pass it instead, as `sx`'s `bgcolor`
takes it: a theme palette path such as `attached="background.paper"`, or any CSS color.

## Accessibility

- Tab reaches the tab list once (on the selected tab), then the selected tab's close button, the add
  button and your actions. Inside the tab list, Left and Right arrow move between tabs, Home and End
  jump to the first and last, and Enter or Space selects the focused tab.
- Delete or Backspace on the selected tab closes it, like its close button (announced through
  `aria-keyshortcuts`). After a tab is closed, or the add button goes away, focus moves to the newly
  selected tab instead of being lost.
- The close button is a separate button next to its tab, not inside it, so screen readers and
  touch users reach it as a normal button. The tab list holds only tabs.
- Icon-only tabs need an `aria-label`, as do icon buttons in the actions.
- The overflow button ("More actions") opens its tray as a dialog (`aria-haspopup="dialog"`); focus
  moves into it, and Escape closes it and gives the focus back to the button. The actions that do not
  fit are left out of the bar, not hidden, so the keyboard never lands on something out of sight.
- The bar is a `<header>` with `role="banner"`. With two bars on a page, as in the application
  shell, give the second one an `aria-label` (or `component="nav"`) so the landmarks can be told
  apart.
- Provide a "skip to content" link before the app bar so keyboard users can bypass it, as this portal
  does.
- With `position="fixed"`, add padding so content is not hidden behind the bar.
